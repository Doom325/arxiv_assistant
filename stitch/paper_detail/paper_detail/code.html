Attention Is All You Need: Revisiting Transformer Architectures for Long-Context Reasoning in Multi-Agent Systems

calendar_today March 15, 2026 | arXiv:2603.12345 | cs.AI cs.LG | schedule 18 min read

Abstract

Recent advances in transformer architectures have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in natural language understanding and generation tasks. However, as multi-agent systems grow in complexity and require reasoning over increasingly long contexts, the fundamental limitations of standard attention mechanisms become apparent. In this work, we revisit the core architectural decisions underlying modern transformers and propose a novel hierarchical attention framework specifically designed for long-context reasoning in cooperative multi-agent environments.

Our approach introduces a three-tier attention hierarchy that operates at the token, segment, and document levels, enabling agents to maintain coherent reasoning chains across context windows exceeding 128,000 tokens. We demonstrate that this hierarchical decomposition not only reduces the quadratic computational complexity of standard attention to near-linear scaling, but also improves the quality of inter-agent communication by providing structured information routing between specialized reasoning modules.

We evaluate our framework on a suite of multi-agent benchmarks including collaborative scientific discovery, distributed code generation, and adversarial debate scenarios. Results show consistent improvements of 12-18% on reasoning accuracy metrics compared to baseline transformer architectures, with particularly strong gains on tasks requiring synthesis of information distributed across multiple agent contexts. Furthermore, we provide theoretical analysis showing that our hierarchical attention mechanism provably preserves the representational capacity of full attention while achieving O(n log n) computational complexity.

Our findings suggest that the next generation of multi-agent systems will benefit significantly from attention architectures explicitly designed for structured inter-agent reasoning, rather than simply scaling existing single-agent transformer designs. We release our full implementation, training recipes, and benchmark suite to facilitate reproducible research in this emerging area.

Key Contributions

  • ● Hierarchical attention framework with three tiers (token, segment, document) that reduces standard O(n^2) complexity to O(n log n) while preserving representational capacity.
  • ● Structured information routing between specialized reasoning modules enables more coherent inter-agent communication compared to flat attention baselines.
  • ● 12-18% improvement on multi-agent reasoning benchmarks across collaborative discovery, distributed code generation, and adversarial debate tasks.
  • ● 128K+ token contexts supported with near-linear scaling, making the approach practical for real-world multi-agent deployments.

Limitations & Open Questions

  • ○ Evaluation limited to English-language benchmarks; cross-lingual multi-agent scenarios remain unexplored.
  • ○ The theoretical guarantees assume bounded vocabulary; extensions to continuous embedding spaces need further investigation.
  • ○ Training recipes require 64 A100 GPUs for 72 hours, limiting accessibility for resource-constrained labs.
